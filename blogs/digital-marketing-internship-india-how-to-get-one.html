Digital Marketing Internship in India: How to Get One as a Fresher

Most internship ads ask for experience, and you need an internship to get experience. The way out is a small piece of real work you can point to. Here is how to build it, who to ask and how to tell a useful internship from one that only gives you free labour to the company.

Key takeaways
  • Show up with one small project already done. It answers the question "can you do this?" before you are asked.
  • Approach agencies and small businesses with a specific idea, not a general request.
  • Ask for the tasks, the supervisor and the certificate before you start.
  • An internship that only posts content with no feedback teaches you very little.
Short answer

To get a digital marketing internship as a fresher, build one or two small real projects first, such as a website or a short audit. Then message small agencies and local businesses with a specific idea and a link to your work. A short, specific message with proof gets more replies than a CV alone.

I have seen the same thing many times. Two freshers apply to one agency. One sends a CV. The other sends a two-page audit of the agency's own website. The second one usually gets the call.

Build something first

You need one or two small, real things. A simple website you built. A short SEO audit of a local shop. A 30-day content calendar for a small brand. A set of ad drafts, clearly labelled as practice. Put them on one page you can link to.

It does not have to be big. It has to be finished, and you have to be able to explain every choice in it.

How to approach agencies

  1. Pick 15 to 20 small agencies and local businesses. Large firms get thousands of applications.
  2. Look at their website and social pages and find one thing you can improve.
  3. Write a short message: who you are, the one idea, and a link to your work.
  4. Follow up once after a week. Do not send the same message ten times.

Use AI to speed up the audit, then check every point yourself. A wrong claim in your first message will cost you the reply.

Paid, unpaid and what to ask

Many fresher internships are unpaid or pay a small stipend. That is not always wrong, but it should be a fair trade. Before you say yes, ask:

  • What tasks will I do, and who reviews my work?
  • How long is it, and how many hours a week?
  • Will I work on real client accounts or only practice tasks?
  • Will I get a certificate or letter, and from whom?

Get the answers in writing, even if it is a short email.

Signs of a good internship

  • A named person who gives you feedback each week.
  • A mix of tasks: research, content, reporting and some campaign work.
  • Access to the tools the team uses.
  • A clear end date and something you can show at the end.

Be careful if the role is only posting on social media every day, with no feedback and no end date.

What to put in the first message

Keep it under 120 words. Agency owners read these on a phone between calls, so the point has to show up in the first two lines.

  • Line one: who you are and what you built. Example: "I am a final-year commerce student. I audited your website and found three fixes."
  • Line two: the finding itself. Name one real problem, such as a missing title tag, a slow page or a form that does not send a confirmation.
  • Line three: the link to your work and one clear ask, such as a 15 minute call this week.

Do not write "I am a hardworking fresher looking for an opportunity". It tells them nothing they can check. Send it to a named person, not to a general info address, and follow up once after four or five days. One polite follow-up is normal. Five is not.

What a good first month looks like

A useful internship has a clear shape even when the agency is small. In the first week you should see how the agency works: who the clients are, how tasks are assigned and where files are kept. In weeks two and three you should own a small task from start to finish, such as a keyword list, a month of social posts or a weekly report. In the last week you should present what you did and get feedback.

Keep a simple log from day one. Write the date, the task, the tool you used and the result. At the end of the month you will have a list of real work you can talk about in interviews, and you will not have to remember it from memory.

If you only make coffee, forward emails or post on the agency's own page with no feedback, ask for a different task in week two. If nothing changes, treat it as a lesson and move to a better internship rather than stay for the certificate.

How AI skills help you get picked

Small agencies are short on time, so a fresher who can use AI tools to finish a first draft of an audit, a content plan or a report is useful from the first week. Show it in your project: say which tool you used, what you asked it, what it got wrong and how you fixed it. That last part matters most. It shows you check the output instead of copying it.

What to do in the last week of an internship

The last week decides how much the internship helps you later.

  • Ask your supervisor for feedback in writing, even a short note.
  • Ask if you may mention the work in your portfolio, and agree what to hide, such as client names.
  • Collect proof: screenshots, reports and the final versions of what you made.
  • Ask for a LinkedIn recommendation or a reference you can name.
  • Thank the team and keep in touch with one person once a month.

Many people finish an internship and forget to collect any of this. Two weeks later it is hard to find.

People also ask

Can a fresher get a digital marketing internship?

Yes. Build one or two small real projects first, such as a website or a short audit, then approach small agencies and local businesses with a specific idea and a link to your work.

Are digital marketing internships paid in India?

Some pay a stipend and many do not. Ask about tasks, supervision, hours and the certificate before you start, and get the answers in writing.

How long is a digital marketing internship?

Many run one to three months. AIM includes a 1 month internship at a partner agency, with a certificate issued by the agency.

Do I need a course before an internship?

Not always, but a course that ends with projects gives you something to show. Some agencies take learners who can show a portfolio even without a course.

How do I get a digital marketing internship with no experience?

Build one small real project, such as a simple website or an audit of a local shop, then send a short message to small agencies with a link to it. Proof of work answers the experience question before they ask it.

What should I put in a digital marketing internship application?

A short note, a link to one project and a clear ask. Skip long CVs and general claims. One real finding from an audit of the agency's own site works better than a page of skills.

Is an unpaid digital marketing internship worth it?

It can be, if you get real tasks, feedback and a certificate or reference you can verify. Ask about tasks, hours and supervision before you start. If the work is only errands, it is not worth your time.

How many hours a day does a digital marketing intern work?

It varies by agency. Many interns work four to eight hours a day, and some work part time. Ask for the schedule, the number of weeks and the tasks in writing before you accept.

Can an internship turn into a full-time job?

Sometimes, but nobody can promise it. It happens more often when you take ownership of tasks, ask for feedback and show the agency a result they can measure. Treat it as a possible outcome, not a plan.

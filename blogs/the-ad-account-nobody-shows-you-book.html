What it gives you

  • Steps you can follow today. 75 chapters, each with a step-by-step SOP, plus checklists, templates and interview answers, so you finish tasks instead of just reading about them.

Who it is for: pick your path

You do not read this book front to back. Chapter 1 gives you a reading path for your job:

  • New to ads: start with Chapters 1 to 5, then tracking, targeting and your first Meta campaign.
  • Freelancer: accounts and access, metrics, policies, creative testing, then the freelancing chapter.
  • Agency or in-house team: structure, media planning, scaling, reporting and account audits.
  • Founder: how ads work, the money math, choosing platforms, and how to hire and judge an agency.
  • Job seeker: metrics, structure, troubleshooting, portfolio, interview prep and every interview box.

It is for beginners, marketers, freelancers, founders and agency teams who want to run real budgets, not read theory.

Two volumes, 75 chapters and eight appendices, in eleven parts: foundations; policies and account safety; tracking and data; audiences and targeting; creative and copy; campaign playbooks for each platform; strategy, media planning and scaling; measurement and reporting; AI, automation, MCP and CLI; careers and business; and add-on chapters on lead follow-up, landing pages, creators and regional language ads. The appendices hold an SOP library, checklists, templates, ad specs, a glossary, a prompt library and interview answers.

Why you can trust the numbers

Platform screens and rules change often, so each chapter says the month its facts were checked. When a number is a rule of thumb, the book says so. When it comes from an example account, it says "example", and those accounts are made up for teaching. When we could not confirm something, the book says that too and tells you where to check. Ads advice online mixes platform rules, rules of thumb and opinion. This book keeps them apart.

Try one idea today: the six-column test log

From Chapter 1, and it works even if you never read another page. Keep one spreadsheet with these columns and fill it in every time you change something:

  1. Date
  2. Platform and campaign
  3. What I changed
  4. Why
  5. Numbers before
  6. Numbers after, with the date

In a few weeks you will know what works in your account, backed by your own data instead of memory.

How to read it without getting overwhelmed

With 75 chapters, the trick is not to read it front to back in one go. A better plan is to read in three passes.

  1. Week 1: foundations. Read the account structure and tracking chapters and set up your own test account. Do every step on screen as you read.
  2. Weeks 2 to 4: one platform at a time. Pick the platform your business or target employer uses most and go deep before touching the others.
  3. Afterwards: use it as a reference. Keep it open while working. The chapter format is built so you can find the screen you need in under a minute.

What makes screenshots matter more than theory

Ad platforms change their menus often, and most written advice is out of date within months. A dated, real screenshot tells you exactly what the screen looked like when the instructions were written, so you can see quickly what has moved. It also builds a skill that no theory can: recognising where you are in a platform at a glance. That is what makes experienced marketers look fast.

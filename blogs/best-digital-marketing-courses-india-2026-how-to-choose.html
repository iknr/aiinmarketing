How to choose a digital marketing course: five questions

A higher fee is not the problem. A higher fee that buys the same thing as a cheaper one is. Whatever you are quoted, ask for the same five answers.

1. Is AI one chapter or part of every module?

Companies now use AI in ads, content, SEO, conversion and lead handling. If AI is a single module near the end, you learn about it. If it runs through every module, you learn to work with it. Why it matters: the second gets you noticed in your first week at work.

2. Do you work inside the tools, and how many people share each login?

This is the question almost nobody asks and it changes everything. In our experience, many programs give no tool access at all. Where access is offered, one login shared by 10 to 20 learners, or a handful of accounts for a batch of 40, is a common pattern. With numbers like that you wait for a turn, get locked out mid-task and end up watching someone else click. Ask for the exact number of learners per login. AIM includes pro tools in month 2 and ad spend for your portfolio, Claude, Semrush, Google AI Pro, Canva Pro, Ubersuggest and SpyFu, in small groups so you are doing the work, not queueing for it.

3. Is there real client work?

Practice projects on made-up brands teach you to finish assignments. Briefs shaped like company needs, and a month inside an agency, teach you to do the job. AIM ends with a 1-month agency internship and a certificate from that agency.

4. Can you verify the trainers?

Open their LinkedIn profiles before you pay. AIM’s four trainers have public profiles that show work with brands such as Infosys, Truecaller, Acko, PayU, NoBroker and SIDA Digital, some current and some past, so check the roles and dates yourself. Start with Kautilya Roshan and Kailas Nath R, then see all four on our trainers section.

5. What do you leave with?

A certificate says you attended. A live portfolio, live ad campaigns you have run and agency work say what you can do. We do not promise jobs. We give you the work, the agency month and interview preparation, and every placement we show links to a public profile.

Vibe marketing course in India: what it is and what to learn

Vibe marketing means telling AI what you want in plain language and letting it do much of the building, writing and number-crunching, while you decide what is good. The term is borrowed from vibe coding. Done well, it means a website in hours, a report before lunch, ad accounts that tell you where money is leaking and leads that get answered in seconds.

A good vibe marketing course should give you three things:

  • Hands-on practice with the real tools, such as WordPress with MCP, Claude and the command line, and ads connected through MCPs, so the speed is yours and not a demo.
  • The judgment to check AI output, because fast and wrong is worse than slow and right.
  • Proof you can show, a portfolio built by doing, not slides.

That is what AIM is built around. Read the plain-language guide, what vibe marketing is and how to learn it, for the full picture.

AI marketing course: what a good one should include

An AI marketing course is only useful if AI shows up in the work you actually do. Look for:

  • AI in every channel: websites, SEO and AI search, content, paid ads, analytics and conversion.
  • Automation and chatbots: workflows that capture and qualify leads, so enquiries get a reply in seconds, even at 2 am.
  • Agents with a human in charge: assistants that research and report, with you approving anything that spends money or talks to customers.
  • Guardrails: approved claims, data privacy and checking analysis before acting on it.

If a syllabus says only “AI tools” and “prompt engineering”, keep looking. Our checklist, seven questions to ask before paying for any AI marketing course, goes deeper.

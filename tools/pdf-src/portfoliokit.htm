Why most fresher portfolios get skipped

A person reviewing 40 applications gives each portfolio under a minute. They are looking for four things, and most fresher pages show none of them.

1. A clear problem

Who was this for and what were they trying to do? "Grow Instagram" is not a problem. "A bakery with 300 followers and no online orders" is.

2. What you actually did

Three to five steps in plain words. Not "SEO, SEM, SMM". Say what you changed and why.

3. A number or a before and after

Even small is fine. 41 clicks from a Rs 500 test is real. A screenshot with a date is proof. A claim is not.

4. What you would change

This shows judgement. A fresher who can say what went wrong is more believable than one whose every project "went viral".

No client? That is normal. Every project in this kit can be done on your own project, a friend's small business, a local shop, or a made-up brand with real data you collect yourself. The only rule: be honest about which one it is. Say "my own test site" or "a local shop, with the owner's permission". Never present a practice project as paid client work.

Three honesty rules that protect you

  1. Label every project: own project, practice with permission, or paid work.
  2. Use accounts the business owns. If you run ads for someone, they create the ad account and add you as a partner. Never run ads from your own account and lose the proof when you stop.
  3. Hide private data. Blur customer names, phone numbers and spend you were asked to keep private.

How many projects do you need?

Two finished projects beat six half-done ones. Start with two from the next pages, finish them in 14 days, and add a third only after you have published the first two.

Portfolio KitSix projects · 1 to 3

Six projects you can finish without a client

Each one takes 3 to 7 days. Each one ends with something you can screenshot.

1

Local search audit for a real shop

The task. Pick a local business with a weak Google Business Profile. Audit it and write down what to fix and why.
Do this. Check the name, category, hours, photos, reviews and how the shop shows up for 5 searches a customer would use. Rank 10 findings by impact. If the owner agrees, make the fixes and note the dates.
Tools. Google Search, Google Maps, Google Business Profile (owner access), a spreadsheet.
Proof to show. The 10-finding audit as a one-page PDF. If the owner let you edit: before and after screenshots with dates.
Say in an interview. "I found three fixes that would matter most and I put them first, because the shop had no time for ten."
2

A small site that ranks for one narrow search

The task. Build a 5-page site or blog around one narrow topic, for example "digital marketing for dentists in Kochi".
Do this. Pick 5 to 10 search phrases with low competition. Write one page per phrase. Add the site to Google Search Console. Wait 4 weeks and check which queries show impressions.
Tools. Google Search Console, Google Trends, a free site builder or any simple host, an AI writing tool for drafts that you edit.
Proof to show. Search Console performance chart with dates. Even 50 impressions counts. Show the trend and what you changed after week 2.
Say in an interview. "The first draft got no impressions, so I rewrote two titles to match how people search, and one page started showing."
3

A Rs 500 paid ads test, two creatives

The task. Run a tiny ad test for a real offer, such as a workshop, a community event or a friend's shop.
Do this. Set one goal (leads, not likes). Make two creatives that differ in only one thing, such as the first line. Run both for 5 days at Rs 100 a day. Record the cost per lead and which one won.
Tools. Meta Ads Manager, a Google Form or WhatsApp link for leads, a spreadsheet. The business should own the ad account.
Proof to show. Screenshot of results with the date range, your test setup, and one paragraph on why the winner won.
Say in an interview. "Creative B cost less per lead, but only 9 leads came in, so I would not call it final. I would retest with more budget."

4

A 5-message WhatsApp or email sequence

The task. Write a follow-up sequence for people who enquired about an offer and went quiet.
Do this. Map the 5 reasons a person goes quiet (price, timing, trust, confusion, forgot). Write one message per reason. Send it to 10 real people who agreed to help, or use a friend's enquiry list with permission. Count replies.
Tools. A free email tool or WhatsApp Business, a spreadsheet to log replies.
Proof to show. The 5 messages, who each one is for, the reply count, and the one message you would delete.
Say in an interview. "The trust message got the most replies. People wanted to see a real example, not another offer."
5

One weekly task handed to AI, with a human check

The task. Pick a repeating task, such as a weekly ad report draft, a competitor ad scan or a content calendar, and build a repeatable AI workflow for it.
Do this. Time the task by hand first. Write the prompt with a role, task, context and output format. Run it on 3 weeks of data. List what the AI got wrong. Add a review step before anything goes to a client.
Tools. Any AI assistant, a spreadsheet, a simple checklist.
Proof to show. Time before and after, the prompt, 3 outputs, and the list of errors you caught.
Say in an interview. "It saved about 40 minutes a week, but it made up a number twice, so a person approves every report."
6

A 12-post content series with a result

The task. Publish 12 posts on one topic and learn what the audience actually reacts to.
Do this. Choose one narrow audience. Test 3 hook styles across 12 posts. After 3 weeks, rank the posts by saves and comments, not likes. Write what the top 2 have in common.
Tools. LinkedIn or Instagram, the platform's own analytics, a spreadsheet.
Proof to show. The 12-post table with numbers, the best and worst post side by side, and your next 6 posts based on what you learned.
Say in an interview. "My best post was the plain one with a real example. The clever one got the fewest saves."

Which two should you start with?

Aiming at an SEO or content role: start with 2 and 6.

Aiming at paid ads or performance: start with 3 and 5.

Aiming at an agency or client role: start with 1 and 4.

Not sure yet: start with 1 and 3. Both finish fast and show results.

Portfolio KitCase study template

The one-page case study template

Copy this into a document or a web page. One case study, one page. Fill every row.

Title
What changed, in one line. Example: "Cut cost per lead for a local workshop from Rs 210 to Rs 130 in 5 days".
Label
Own project, practice with permission, or paid work. Month and year.
The problem
Who it was for, what they wanted, and what was not working. Two sentences.
What I did
Three to five steps in plain words. Include the one decision you are proudest of.
Tools
Only the tools you really used.
Result
Numbers with the date range and where they came from. If the sample is small, say so.
What went wrong
One thing that failed, and what you did next.
What I would do next
One test you would run with more time or budget.
Proof
List the screenshots, files or links. Add the date to each.
Worked sample. Numbers below are made up to show the format. Use your own.
Title: Two ad creatives, one workshop, Rs 500 budget. Label: Practice with permission, Nov 2026.
Problem: A weekend photography workshop had 40 seats and 9 sign-ups, and the organiser had only ever posted organically.
What I did: Ran two Meta ads for 5 days at Rs 100 a day. Same photo, different first line: one about the price, one about the skill gained. Sent clicks to a WhatsApp chat.
Result: Price line: 14 chats, Rs 36 each. Skill line: 22 chats, Rs 23 each. Eight chats became sign-ups.
What went wrong: Four chats asked the venue address, which was missing from the ad. I added it on day 3.
Next: Test a video testimonial against the photo with a Rs 1,500 budget.
Proof: 3 screenshots of results (dated), both creatives, the chat count sheet.

Portfolio KitChecklist and interview

The 12-point portfolio page checklist

☐ One link to share. Put it in your resume header and LinkedIn Featured.
☐ The first screen says who you are, what you do and who you help, in two lines.
☐ The page opens in about 2 seconds on a phone.
☐ 2 to 4 case studies, not 12. Best one first.
☐ Every case study has a number, a date range and a label (own, practice, paid).
☐ Screenshots are dated, and private data is blurred.
☐ Each case study says what went wrong or what you would change.
☐ A short "how I work" paragraph with the tools you really use.
☐ A WhatsApp or email contact that works.
☐ A PDF copy of the same page for recruiters who ask for attachments.
☐ No stock photos of laptops and handshakes.
☐ Updated in the last 90 days, with the month shown.

10 questions about your own projects

1. Why did you pick this project?Tie it to the role you want. One sentence.
2. What did not work?Name it plainly, then say the change you made.
3. How do you know your work caused the result?Say what else could explain it, and what you checked.
4. What was the budget and who paid?Be exact. Honest small beats vague big.
5. Walk me through the numbers.Know every figure on the page, and where it came from.
6. What would you do with Rs 50,000?Name one test, why it comes first and what result would stop you.
7. Which part did AI do?Say it openly, and say what you checked by hand.
8. What would you do differently?One concrete change, not "everything".
9. What did the client or owner think?If there was feedback, quote it. If there was none, say so.
10. What did you learn that surprised you?The best answers are small and specific.

Portfolio Kit14-day plan

A 14-day plan for two finished projects

Part-time pace, about 1.5 hours a day. Pick the two projects from page 4 first.

Day 1
Pick two projects and the audience for each. Write the label (own, practice, paid) for both.
Day 2
Set up the tools and tracking. Create a spreadsheet for numbers. Take "before" screenshots with dates.
Days 3 to 5
Project A: do the work and start the test or audit. Log numbers every day.
Days 6 to 8
Project B: do the work. Keep logging both.
Day 9
Check results. Write down what went wrong in each project while it is fresh.
Days 10 to 11
Write both case studies with the template. Cut every sentence that has no number or no step.
Day 12
Build the one page that holds them. Go through the 12-point checklist.
Day 13
Ask one person to read it for 60 seconds and tell you what they remember. Fix what they missed.
Day 14
Publish. Post one case study on LinkedIn as a story, with the lesson first. Add the link to your resume.
After day 14. Apply with the link in your first line. In every message, name the one case study closest to the role, and say what number moved.

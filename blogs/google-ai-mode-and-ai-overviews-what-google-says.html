Google AI Mode and AI Overviews: What Google Actually Says, and a Checklist

Many guides promise secret tricks for AI Mode and AI Overviews. Google has published its own advice, and it is simpler and less exciting. This guide sums up what Google says, what it says you do not need, and gives you a 10-point checklist you can use this week.

Key takeaways
  • Google says there are no extra requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond the normal Search requirements.
  • You do not need special AI files, special markup or content chopped into small pieces.
  • What helps is original, clear, helpful pages, good technical basics and accurate business details.
  • Traffic from these features is counted in your normal Search Console web results.
Short answer

To appear in Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to show a snippet in normal Search. Google says no extra requirements, special markup or AI-specific files are needed. Focus on original, clear content, good technical basics, internal links and accurate business details.

What Google says in its own guidance

Google has two pages that matter here: one on AI features and your website, and one on optimising for generative AI features in Search. Here is what they say, in plain words.

  • Eligibility: a page can appear as a supporting link if it is indexed and eligible to show with a snippet in Search. There are no additional requirements for AI Overviews or AI Mode.
  • No special markup: you do not need new machine-readable files, AI text files or special structured data.
  • Normal SEO still applies: let Google crawl the site, link pages to each other, keep important content as text and give people a good page experience.
  • Structured data: if you use it, keep it consistent with what the page shows.
  • Business details: keep Google Business Profile and Merchant Center information accurate.
  • Measurement: traffic from these features is included in the Web search type in Search Console.

Google also notes that meeting every requirement does not guarantee a page will be crawled, indexed or shown.

What Google says you do not need

This part saves time and money. In its optimisation guide, Google says Search does not need or use the following for these features.

  • Special AI files such as llms.txt, or Markdown copies of your pages.
  • Cutting content into small pieces for AI systems.
  • Rewriting content only for AI systems, since they understand related words and meaning.
  • Manufacturing brand mentions across the web.
  • Focusing only on structured data, which AI features do not require.
  • Making many pages for tiny variations of the same query.

We keep an llms.txt file on this site because it costs little and some other tools may read it. But Google says its Search does not use it, so do not count on it to bring traffic.

A 10-point checklist for your site

  1. Indexed: inspect your key pages in Search Console. Fix pages that are discovered or crawled but not indexed.
  2. Snippet allowed: make sure you have not blocked snippets with nosnippet or a very low max-snippet on pages you want shown.
  3. Crawl access: check robots.txt and any CDN or security layer so Googlebot can reach your pages.
  4. Text first: keep the main answer as readable text, not only in images or scripts.
  5. Direct answers: answer the question in the first sentences, then add detail below.
  6. Original value: add first-hand detail, examples, steps, screenshots or data that generic pages do not have.
  7. Clear authors and sources: show who wrote the page and where facts come from.
  8. Internal links: link related pages together so they are easy to find.
  9. Accurate details: keep name, address, phone, hours, prices and offers correct on your site and profiles.
  10. Page experience: fast loading, readable on mobile and no pop-ups that cover the content.

None of these are new. That is the point: the work that helps in AI features is the work that helps in normal search.

How to write a page that earns a place in AI answers

Nobody outside Google can promise a citation, but you can make a page worth quoting.

  • Use the customer's own question as a heading, and answer it in two or three plain sentences.
  • Follow with the details: steps, a short list or a table, and one real example.
  • Say what you know first-hand. For example, "In our program we do X" is more useful than a general line any site could copy.
  • Give a date and update the page when facts change.
  • Avoid claims you cannot support, since weak claims reduce trust with readers and with systems that rate quality.

How to measure whether it is working

Google says AI feature traffic is included in your overall Search data in the Performance report. That means you will not see a clean AI Mode line item in every report, so track a few things yourself.

  • Impressions and clicks for your key pages and queries over time in Search Console.
  • Branded searches, which show whether more people look for your name.
  • Enquiries, calls and messages, and a question on the form: "How did you find us?"
  • A monthly check: ask a few AI tools the questions your customers ask, and record whether you are mentioned and what is said.

Write the results in a sheet with the date and the tool. After three months you will see a trend, even if no single number is exact.

Steps for a local or small business

If you run a clinic, a shop, a tuition centre or a small agency, start with these.

  • Complete your Google Business Profile with the right category, services, hours and photos.
  • Ask real customers for reviews and reply to each one.
  • Write one page for each main service, with a clear price range or a clear way to ask.
  • Add a page that answers the five questions customers ask most.
  • Check that your phone, address and name match everywhere.

A 30-day plan

  • Week 1: run the 10-point checklist on your top five pages and fix technical issues.
  • Week 2: rewrite the first paragraph of each page to answer the main question directly.
  • Week 3: add one first-hand example, one source and one author line to each page.
  • Week 4: complete your business profile, ask for reviews and record your first AI answer check.

Repeat the checks monthly. Small, steady updates beat one big rewrite.

People also ask

How do I get my website into Google AI Mode and AI Overviews?

Make sure the page is indexed and eligible to show a snippet in Google Search. Google says there are no additional requirements. Then improve the page with original, clear content, internal links and accurate business details.

Do I need special markup or schema for AI Overviews?

No. Google says no special structured data is required. If you use markup, keep it consistent with the text on the page.

Does llms.txt help with Google AI Overviews or AI Mode?

Google says it does not use llms.txt or similar AI text files for Search. Other tools may read them, but do not rely on one for Google traffic.

Is traffic from AI Mode shown in Search Console?

Google says traffic from AI Overviews and AI Mode is included in the overall Search data, under the Web search type. You may need to track page, query and enquiry trends to judge impact.

Should I rewrite my content for AI?

Google says rewriting content only for AI systems is not needed. Write for people: clear answers, first-hand detail, accurate facts and good structure.

Can small businesses appear in AI answers?

Yes. Small businesses can appear when their pages and profiles are clear, accurate and helpful. Complete your Google Business Profile, collect real reviews and answer customer questions on your site.

You do not need a marketing degree or any coding to start. You do need a course that teaches in an order that makes sense, lets you practise from the first week and ends with something you can show. Here is how to pick one when you are starting from zero.

Key takeaways
  • A beginner course should start with how marketing works, then move into tools, not the other way round.
  • You learn faster when you build a real website and run a real task each week.
  • Three months of steady practice is a fair length for a first course. Be careful with promises of mastery in two weeks.
  • Check the total fee, who teaches, and what you will have built at the end.
Short answer

A beginner digital marketing course should start from the basics, let you practise in real tools from week one and end with a project you can show. It takes about three months of regular classes. You do not need a marketing degree or coding. Check that classes are live, the tools are yours to log into and the refund rule is written down.

We teach beginners every month, so this comes from that. I think the biggest worry is not the tools. It is not knowing what to learn first, and in what order. So that is where we begin.

What a beginner needs first

Three things. A clear picture of how marketing brings in customers, a few tools you can use without fear, and one finished project you made yourself. Everything else can come later.

You do not need to know every channel on day one. Pick the ones that matter most for jobs and freelance work: websites and SEO, social media and content, and paid ads. Add AI to each of them, because companies now expect you to work with it.

A sensible order to learn in

  1. Websites. Build a simple site on WordPress. It teaches you how pages, forms and tracking fit together, and it becomes your portfolio.
  2. Search and AI search. Keywords, on-page basics and how people now find answers in AI tools.
  3. Social media and content. A 30-day content calendar, captions, and simple automations for messages.
  4. Paid ads. Campaign structure, audiences, budgets and how to read a report.
  5. Planning and reporting. Putting numbers behind a plan so you can explain it to a client.

A good course repeats one idea in each step: use AI to go faster, then check the result yourself. That check is the part most beginners skip, and it is where mistakes happen.

How long it takes

For a first course, three months of regular classes and project work is a fair length. At AIM it is 8 weeks of training and 4 weeks of agency internship, with live classes Monday to Friday, 9 to 11 pm IST. Weekends are for doubts, project reviews and interview prep. That suits people who study or work during the day.

If a course says you will be job ready in two weeks, I would be careful. Skills build with practice, and practice takes time.

Tools you will meet early

An AI assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT, WordPress, Google Search Console and GA4, a design tool such as Canva, and one SEO tool such as Semrush. Many have free plans. Our higher plan adds tool access, but the training is the same in both plans, so a tight budget does not stop you from learning.

Mistakes beginners make

  • Watching videos for weeks without building anything.
  • Collecting certificates and having no project to show.
  • Trusting an AI answer without checking it.
  • Choosing the cheapest or the most expensive course without asking what is included.

Checks before you pay

  • Are classes live, and can you ask questions in them?
  • Will you log in to the real tools yourself?
  • What will you have built when it ends?
  • Can you see the trainers' public profiles?
  • What is the full fee and the refund rule, in writing?

For more detail, read our guide on what to ask before you pay for an AI marketing course, and compare options with the best digital marketing courses in India guide.

What your first month can look like

A beginner does best with a small weekly target. Here is a plan you can follow with any course, or on your own.

  • Week 1: learn how a customer finds a business and buys. Write one page that explains it for a business you know.
  • Week 2: build a simple website on WordPress with a home page, a service page and a contact form.
  • Week 3: set up Google Analytics and Search Console on that site and check that they receive data.
  • Week 4: write five posts or ads for a real or sample business and ask someone to review them.

At the end you will have a site, a tracking setup and a set of drafts. That is already more than most beginners show in their first interview.

Using AI as a beginner without learning the wrong habits

AI tools make a beginner look faster, but they can also hide gaps. Use them this way.

  • Ask AI to explain a topic, then explain it back in your own words.
  • Let AI write a first draft, then edit it and note what you changed.
  • Check any number, claim or date it gives you against the real source.
  • Keep a short list of prompts that worked, with the task and the result.

A good course teaches this from week one. If AI shows up only in a final chapter, you will spend months building habits without it.

Which path to choose after the basics

After the first few weeks, you will notice which tasks you enjoy. People who like numbers lean towards ads and analytics. People who like writing and design lean towards content and branding. People who like building lean towards websites, automation and AI workflows.

You do not have to pick on day one. AIM teaches the core skills first, then four specialisations from week 5 to week 8: AI Generalist, Marktech, Branding and Content, and Performance and Growth. You choose with real project work behind you.

Questions to ask a counsellor on your first call

A first call is easier with a list. Take these with you.

  • What will I have built by week four, and can I see an example from a recent learner?
  • How many hours a week are live, and how many are practice with feedback?
  • Which tools will I log into myself, and are they free, paid or shared?
  • Who are the trainers, and where can I see their work?
  • What is the total fee with GST, and what is the refund rule?
  • What happens if I miss a class?

Write the answers down and compare them with another course. If a counsellor pushes you to pay today, step back. A good course will still be there tomorrow.

Should I learn AI tools as a beginner?

Yes, from the start. Companies expect marketers to work with AI, and it is easier to learn the right habits early, including how to check what the tool gives you.

Can a beginner learn digital marketing in 3 months?

Yes, you can learn the basics and finish a first project in three months with regular classes and practice. Skill after that grows with real work, so an internship helps.

Do I need to know coding for digital marketing?
